## How the documentation is organized[¶](#how-the-documentation-is-organized "Permalink to this headline")

Django has a lot of documentation. A high-level overview of how it’s organized will help you know where to look for certain things:

-   [Tutorials](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/2.0/intro/) take you by the hand through a series of steps to create a Web application. Start here if you’re new to Django or Web application development. Also look at the “[First steps](#index-first-steps)” below.
-   [Topic guides](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/2.0/topics/) discuss key topics and concepts at a fairly high level and provide useful background information and explanation.
-   [Reference guides](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/2.0/ref/) contain technical reference for APIs and other aspects of Django’s machinery. They describe how it works and how to use it but assume that you have a basic understanding of key concepts.
-   [How-to guides](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/2.0/howto/) are recipes. They guide you through the steps involved in addressing key problems and use-cases. They are more advanced than tutorials and assume some knowledge of how Django works.

## Geographic framework[¶](#geographic-framework "Permalink to this headline")

[GeoDjango](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/2.0/ref/contrib/gis/) intends to be a world-class geographic Web framework. Its goal is to make it as easy as possible to build GIS Web applications and harness the power of spatially enabled data.
